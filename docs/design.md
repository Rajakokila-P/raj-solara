# Design rationale and student evaluation

This rationale describes the generated initial implementation, not independent student research.

## Intended experience

The hero introduces a clearly fictional solar-assisted aircraft. The collection establishes three mission types. Comparison exposes the assumptions. The energy lab makes trade-offs interactive. Technology text describes limitations before the studio invites customisation.

Midnight blue suggests sky and technical detail; white provides a calm reading surface; British red identifies actions. Large headings establish hierarchy. The original top-view SVG communicates the wing solar surfaces and changes with the chosen finish/interior. All key content remains available without hover. Native controls, labels, focus indicators and reduced-motion styles support accessibility. Cards stack on small screens and comparison tables scroll within a labelled region.

## Initial layout map

Desktop: header; split text/aircraft hero; four collection cards; comparison; two-column energy lab; technology selector; split preview/studio; enquiry; footer.
Mobile: wrapping navigation; stacked hero; single-column cards; contained comparison scroll; stacked energy controls/results; stacked studio and enquiry.

## BR101 revision rationale

The user supplied a 14-slide presentation. The revised hero names BR101 and the first content section makes its four-stage systems loop readable as ordinary HTML. Six native buttons reveal explanations without hover-only controls. Original slide artwork is inside a user-opened details panel with an accessible description. British navy, white and red preserve the requested identity while silver remains part of the proposed airframe narrative.

The two new exercises distinguish modelled energy from verified performance. A readable hourly table avoids conveying results through colour alone. Mission-update loading, cancellation, selection changes and failure have visible status feedback. Request versioning prevents stale delayed results. These are design intentions; keyboard, assistive-technology and real-device evaluation remain necessary.
